# Advertising message, claims and review

Use this for advertising or marketing work. It covers what the film says and whether it is true, how product, copy and UI appear on screen, and the review before delivery. How the idea itself is built belongs to [marketing idea](marketing-idea.md). Other stories and films keep their own creative goals.

## Build or read the brand context

Product facts come from the advertiser, never from the skill or the reference video. They live in the project's `brand/brand-context.md`, shared by every film for this brand. When it exists, read it and check it is current. When it is missing, ask the user for their product material, such as a growth plan, product facts, store screenshots and brand assets, and build it with these parts.

- The product and what makes it different, with the screenshots that show each feature.
- The audience, markets, languages and channels.
- The positioning line and the key messages, each with what it says and what the advertiser wants to learn from it.
- The claim limits of each message.
- The brand assets available and where the exact UI comes from.

Show the file to the user before relying on it. When the user corrects a product fact or a claim limit, write the correction into this file.

## Establish this film's message and its limits

From the brand context and the request, record in the film's brief the audience situation, campaign purpose, the one key message this film carries, desired viewer action, language, platform and length, and any required UI or copy. A key message is the main thing the advertiser wants this audience to understand or remember. Follow the campaign's chosen message count; when it is unspecified, recommend one message per film rather than packing in every feature.

For each claim used in the script, record its source and material conditions. Distinguish live functionality, planned capability, the advertiser's hypothesis and unknown information. Exact pricing, guaranteed outcomes, free use, eligibility and remembered context must match supplied or verified facts. A short caption cannot quietly remove the condition that makes a claim true. Meaning-changing ambiguity is a targeted question or a clearly provisional alternative, not permission to choose the most exciting claim.

Everyday phrases and memes that describe the viewer's own situation are not product claims. Before removing one, check whether a viewer would read it as a promise about the product. When that reading is uncertain, keep the phrase and mark the question for the advertiser.

Mark new fictional scenarios and compressed source events. Never present a concept UI or invented feature as a product recording.

## Product, copy and UI on screen

Keep review annotations, missing-asset notes and test status outside the audience's copy. Do not turn internal statements such as "successful example shown" into automatic captions. Where an audience-facing disclosure is needed to prevent a misleading product implication, write concise viewer language and give it readable space. A review-copy concept label can identify an unfinished mockup; it does not establish a publishable script or replace verification of the portrayed feature.

Use the approved logo, UI and materials. Mark how precise elements will be realized, such as supplied screen recording, post-production overlay or explicitly authorized concept design. Do not assume the image or video model can reproduce exact UI, brand marks or typography. An unexplained end card cannot repair a script whose product promise is unclear.

For product UI shown inside the film, take layouts, images, fonts, colours and motion timings from the product's own design files or client source when the user can provide them, and compute any displayed values with the product's real rules. Record the source of each element. Screenshots are a fallback; redrawn UI is a labelled last resort. Show the screen with the product's own layout, layering and flow. Do not shrink, move or redraw it to fit the story, and do not cut away to it as a separate recording when the product itself shows it over the scene.

If the ending asks for an action, match the action and destination actually available. Follow the user's requested branding prominence and campaign format; do not force a sales line into every organic post.

## Review before delivery

Read the actual sequence without its explanatory notes, once as a viewer who knows nothing about the story or product and once against the brief.

- Would someone who loves the stories this audience loves see, in the first seconds, a story she would choose to watch?
- Can the film be told as one event in one sentence, and does every shot, sound and passage of music serve that event's feeling?
- Would these people, in this place, really do what the film shows, by the evidence gathered, and does any beat depend on something the viewer was never shown?
- Does the ending stop on an image, or does a line say what the moment means?
- Does the film use an approach the user has rejected in the brand record, or repeat an idea an earlier film used?
- If a reference was used, are the chosen dimensions recognizable, and have excluded dimensions leaked into appearance or story?
- Are brand, UI and copy accurate, readable, backed by facts including the conditions shown, true to how the product behaves and achievable to produce?
- Does the sound follow [storyboard](storyboard.md#early-feasibility-check) and fit the reveal and action speeds, and which observations remain unverified?

Revise concrete failures before delivery. Record remaining uncertainties and the reason for the recommended direction. Do not certify a script as viral, high-converting or objectively tasteful.

## Keep learning interpretable

Record the script version, message, idea, reference dimensions if any, creative change and intended campaign measure. When results arrive, separate paid from organic reach, platform, audience, spend and downstream behavior, and keep causal conclusions tentative when several things changed or evidence is thin. Corrections about this brand go into the brand files, not into universal rules.
