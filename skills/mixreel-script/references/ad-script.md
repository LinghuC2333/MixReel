# Write an advertising adaptation

Use this only for advertising or marketing work. Other stories and films keep their own creative goals. Read the advertiser's actual material before choosing a creative direction; do not invent its product, audience or purchase rationale from the reference video.

## Establish the message and its limits

Extract the audience's relevant situation, campaign purpose, selected key message(s), desired viewer action, language/platform/length, brand assets and required UI or copy. A key message is the main thing the advertiser wants this audience to understand or remember. Follow the campaign's chosen message count; when it is unspecified, recommend a manageable focus rather than packing in every feature.

For each claim used in the script, record its source and material conditions. Distinguish live functionality, planned capability, the advertiser's hypothesis and unknown information. Exact pricing, guaranteed outcomes, free use, eligibility and remembered context must match supplied or verified facts. A short caption cannot quietly remove the condition that makes a claim true. Meaning-changing ambiguity is a targeted question or a clearly provisional alternative, not permission to choose the most exciting claim.

Keep private growth plans, customer names and product examples in the project. The public skill does not prescribe an audience, business model, visual identity or provider. Confirmed product facts can persist across projects only through the user's supplied, current profile.

## Find an experience that expresses the message

Connect the message to something visible or audible that this viewer would want, recognize, question or react to. Use available product demonstrations, source-story events or authorized new scenarios. Mark new fictional scenarios and compressed source events; never present a concept UI or invented feature as a product recording.

Choose a format that fits the borrowed mechanism and campaign intent. A demonstration, montage, conversation, joke, subjective viewpoint or continuous scene can all be appropriate. Do not force every ad through a fixed problem/solution/twist/CTA template or require every scene to explain a feature. If the advertiser wants a pure emotional teaser, keep that choice and show how it supports the requested goal.

For an unfamiliar audience, make the immediate desire or situation legible before relying on character names and lore. Dialogue should perform an action such as refusing, teasing, persuading or revealing; avoid having characters recite the product specification.

## Show why this audience would stay

For the proposed execution record a short, evidence-linked account: whose viewpoint the viewer occupies; what that audience wants, fears or recognizes according to the brief; the exact opening stimulus; the expectation it creates; and the later action that pays it off or deliberately changes it. Connect the reference's observed event/time to the new event. Distinguish user-confirmed preference, observed reference technique and the agent's creative hypothesis. Do not treat a demographic label as evidence of a desire or invent universal genre tastes.

Check where agency lies when it matters to the brief: who chooses, who acts, who bears a cost, and who receives the payoff. Test the actual event against recorded audience preferences before polishing its wording. A clearly rejected emotional premise fails even when the storyboard format and product facts are correct. Keep project-specific preferences out of public universal rules.

For advertising, explain why the product interaction belongs at that exact moment and what observable consequence follows it. If the product only appears as a logo after a self-contained invented story, mark that integration as weak unless the brief explicitly calls for that treatment. Conversely, do not turn a product insert into a forced demonstration in a deliberately emotional brand film. State an observable creative test or unresolved taste question, not a self-awarded "catchy" score or predicted conversion.

## Compare actual creative directions

When comparing options, vary the opening situation, viewer perspective, reveal or product integration in a meaningful way. State which reference dimensions each preserves, the central message, expected audience response and a trade-off. If a single execution is requested, develop it without manufacturing alternatives.

Write enough actual beats/copy to judge the difference. Choose a direction using fit to the user's objective, reference mechanism and available evidence. This is a creative recommendation, not a prediction of advertising performance.

## Write the film people will watch

Use a timed table or readable sequence with image/action/camera, exact visible text, spoken words and sound. Separate production notes from audience-facing content. Include the opening and ending, meaningful state changes and any intentional montage discontinuity. Leave readable time for text and speech in the target language; count-based estimates require an actual read-through before final timing.

Keep review annotations, missing-asset notes and test status outside the audience's copy. Do not turn internal statements such as "successful example shown" into automatic captions. Where an audience-facing disclosure is needed to prevent a misleading product implication, write concise viewer language and give it readable space. A review-copy concept label can identify an unfinished mockup; it does not establish a publishable script or replace verification of the portrayed feature. Distinguish that review-only treatment from the intended final execution.

Plan when the viewer recognizes the product and why it belongs in this sequence. Use the approved logo/UI/materials. Mark how precise elements will be realized, such as supplied screen recording, post-production overlay or explicitly authorized concept design. Do not assume the image/video model can reproduce exact UI, brand marks or typography. An unexplained end card cannot repair a script whose product promise is unclear.

For product UI shown inside the film, take layouts, images, fonts, colours and motion timings from the product's own design files or client source when the user can provide them, and compute any displayed values with the product's real rules. Record the source of each element. Screenshots are a fallback; redrawn UI is a labelled last resort.

If the ending asks for an action, match the action and destination actually available. Follow the user's requested branding prominence and campaign format; do not force a sales line into every organic post.

## Review before delivery

Read the actual sequence without its explanatory notes, once as an unfamiliar viewer and once against the brief. Ask:

- Does the opening contain a concrete reason to continue, and does a later event fulfill or meaningfully develop that expectation?
- What changes from beat to beat? Remove redundant explanation and decorative shots that delay the intended effect.
- Is the selected product experience understandable from the presented images, words or sound, without relying on the rationale paragraph?
- Does the humor/emotion depend on unsupported familiarity, unearned intimacy or a borrowed trend that this audience may not know?
- Are the chosen reference dimensions recognizable, and have excluded dimensions leaked into appearance or story?
- Are brand/UI/copy natural to the approved execution, accurate, readable and assigned an achievable production method?
- Do actual facts support every implied capability and outcome, including conditions shown in the demonstration?
- Does the new audio fit the script's reveal and action speeds? Which observations remain unverified?

Revise concrete failures before delivery. Record remaining uncertainties and the reason for the recommended direction. Do not certify a script as viral, high-converting or objectively tasteful.

## Keep learning interpretable

Record the script version, selected message, reference dimensions, creative change and intended campaign measure. If the advertiser supplies results later, distinguish paid/organic reach, platform, audience, spend and downstream behavior. When several variables changed together or evidence is sparse, keep causal conclusions uncertain. Feedback from the user updates this private project's choices; it does not automatically become a universal public rule.
