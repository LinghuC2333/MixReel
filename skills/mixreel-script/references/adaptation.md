# Decide what to borrow

Treat the reference as evidence of a particular viewing experience. Read the advertiser/creative brief first, then relate observed choices to that intent. One or more dimensions may be selected. Do not require copying the entire video or automatically excluding its content or script.

## Record the selection

For each relevant dimension record the source time/evidence, the useful mechanism, keep/adapt/exclude/pending, the intended target treatment, reason, and decision authority. In an authorized autonomous proposal the agent may select dimensions and explain its choices; do not label that choice user-confirmed. Preserve already recorded decisions.

| Dimension | What to examine |
| --- | --- |
| Content and composition | Observable event, subject positions/scale, spatial contrast, visual reveal, viewpoint |
| Script and information | What viewers know or expect at each point, exact copy/dialogue, order of reveals, cause/reaction, what the ending resolves or withholds |
| Hook | First noticed stimulus, immediate reason to care, the question or expectation it opens, and the later payoff |
| Music and sound | Phrase, energy, vocals, lyric meaning, silence, recognizable sound or spoken delivery |
| Editing and motion | Cut spacing, pauses, acceleration, action accents, camera/performance behavior and their musical relationship |
| Style and presentation | Rendering, palette, lighting, typography, UI treatment, meme/genre conventions and audience familiarity |

Use the dimensions relevant to this request; the table is not a quota. Exact reference treatment remains subordinate to the user's recorded brand, identity, wardrobe, product and rights constraints. A conflict should produce a clear trade-off, not an unnoticed change. A borrowed composition does not automatically supply style.

## Explain attention through events

Describe the first meaningful event and what a viewer anticipates next. Identify which shot, word, gesture or sound changes that expectation. Locate the payoff and whether it arrives before attention is likely to drift; mark that judgment as a hypothesis, not a measured retention result.

Write the actual copy or action involved when available. Labels such as fast-paced, viral, emotional or cinematic do not explain the mechanism. Do not add fashionable slang to imitate fluency. Check whether a joke, character cameo or nostalgic reference requires knowledge the target viewer lacks. Adapt or replace that dependency explicitly.

## Creative logic and musical timing

A source script's realized pauses, cuts and reveals are tied to its sound. Its underlying creative idea may survive a new track. Preserve the selected idea while rebuilding the timeline against target phrases, accents, spoken readability and action duration. Do not mechanically scale all source timestamps or require a cut at every beat.

When the lyric, recognizable sound or silence is itself the joke or reveal, record that dependency. A replacement track may require a rewritten hook or payoff. A user-selected excerpt stays fixed unless a conflict is surfaced. With no target audio, deliver a stated pacing proposal and leave exact musical alignment pending.

Keep source observation, planned transfer and final asset conditioning separate. For example, a creator's recorded reaction can inspire the new reveal order without becoming a submitted identity or style reference. Record the actual roles again at the image/video stage.

## Evidence boundaries

Document captions, thumbnails and frame sheets are partial evidence. Do not infer an entire shot's motion or sound from them. If a required dimension is unobservable, identify it before claiming the script reproduces it. User preference and supplied campaign results can guide choices; view counts alone do not prove conversion, paid reach, or which creative element caused performance.
