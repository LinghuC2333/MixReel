# Choose a story the viewer can read

## Candidate evidence

Read relevant source passages in context. For each candidate record the chapter or source locator, relationship state at that moment, visible event, obstacle, reaction and final unresolved or resolved feeling. Separate exact source events, compressed events and proposed inventions. Follow the user's adaptation allowance; do not present an invented event as canon.

Start with the reference's intended feeling and the target music's available time. Usually two or three genuinely different candidates suffice. If none works, explain the missing ingredient and propose a source-grounded simplification. Do not manufacture a quota of weak alternatives.

Use these questions to compare candidates.

| Criterion | Evidence to provide |
|---|---|
| Emotional match | The specific look, withheld action or consequence that creates the reference's intended feeling |
| Visible tension | What a viewer unfamiliar with the IP can infer from actions, distance, clothing or gaze |
| Source fidelity | Passage locator and what adaptation changes |
| Timing | Essential beats that can read within the chosen music |
| Filmability | A feasible way to show the action and any difficult contact or prop |
| Visual variation | Distinct shot functions and opportunities for foreground, scale or reveal |

A score may summarize a comparison, but cannot compensate for an absent emotional mechanism. State the strongest objection to the recommended candidate before presenting it.

## Tension without slogans

For romance, specify who wants proximity, what prevents or complicates it, who notices first, and what visible possibility remains open. Uncertainty can come from social risk, separation, conflict, concealment or incompatible choices. Established couples can still carry tension; being unmarried or not yet together is not a universal eligibility rule.

For suspense, specify what the viewer sees before the character, what may happen and which visual evidence supports it. For action, specify the imbalance, threatened outcome and visible reversal. Other genres need their own mechanism. Do not retrofit all stories to romance.

Distinguish dramatic pressure from activity. A political crowd admiring a leader does not automatically imply romantic attraction. A large magical effect does not itself establish a personal stake. A close-up becomes useful when it reveals a response to something legible.

## Cold-viewer check

Read only the proposed visual descriptions, hiding all lore and explanatory notes. Can an unfamiliar viewer infer the intended relationship and change? If the essential information remains invisible, supply a source-grounded cue, remove that dependency or choose another scene.

Complex props earn their place by showing an understandable state change. Removing an unnecessary disguise or token may reduce explanation and model difficulty. Do not remove a plot-critical prop merely because one model struggles; consider a clearer shot or another method.

Keep public examples original and clearly labelled synthetic. Private project histories, customer data and reference artwork do not belong in this distributable skill.
